Since 2021, Anthropy has sought to cultivate intellectual curiosity and meaningful engagement within
the humanities and social sciences. Through initiatives such as Humanities Focus Week and our
flagship In2Academia research programme, we provide students with opportunities to engage
rigorously with academic ideas, learn from experts across disciplines, and pursue independent research
under dedicated mentorship.

It is therefore with great pride that we present the third edition of the Anthropy Review. This journal
showcases the work of budding student researchers who have spent months developing original
questions, engaging with academic literature, and refining their arguments in their first time through
the research process. The articles that follow reflect not only the breadth of interests within our
community, but also the intellectual ambition, curiosity, and diligence that underpin scholarly inquiry.

This issue presents a diverse range of research, each paper offering a fresh perspective on complex
topics related to identity, culture, and power. Anahita Ghosh Singh (UWCSEA) examines how the
Abortion Act 1967 acted as a paternalistic compromise that restricted bodily autonomy through medical
gatekeeping. Derek Shih (UWCSEA) traces the dramatic shift in EU trade relations and integration,
showing how U.S. tariff shocks forced the bloc toward strategic autonomy and deeper economic
interdependence despite internal political constraints. Leyan Ji (UWCSEA) highlights the pivotal role of
economic dissatisfaction and stagnation in driving right-wing nationalism in Japan. Katherine Wang
(UWC Changshu China) explores the impact of crop straw burning on air quality and resident wellbeing
in Baguazhou, Nanjing. And Yueqi Gou (UWCSEA) provides an analysis of how intrinsic motivation and
growth mindsets interact with patient non-adherence among hypertensive patients.

This journal represents a three-month process of dedication and collaboration, with ten sessions
devoted to refining each paper through feedback and discussion. We thank all the mentors who
devoted their energy and time to guiding the students throughout this process — their support has
been invaluable in shaping these papers and ensuring the quality of this journal.
