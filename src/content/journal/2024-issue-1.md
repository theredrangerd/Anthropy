Since 2021, Anthropy has provided an outreach platform for students to foster early intellectual curiosity and
meaningful engagement within the humanities and social sciences. We empower students to explore their research
interests with academic rigor, connect with experts, and share their insights with the broader community, through
signature events such as Humanities Focus Week and our flagship In2Academia research programme.

It is with great pride that we present the inaugural edition of the Anthropy Review, a journal celebrating the
outstanding work of our first cohort of student researchers.

This issue's papers span a wide range of subjects. Marcus Lu traces the history of tolerance and suppression of
homosexuality in China, from imperial-era acceptance to modern censorship, in "From 'Passions of the Cut Sleeve' to
LGBT Censorship: How China 'Learned' Homophobia." Alex Wondra examines the tension between climate action and
profit-driven production in "The Struggle Between the Need for Climate Action and the Drive to Make Profit." Jimmy Ji
interrogates the ethics of taxation in "Is Tax a Legal Disguise for Theft?" Alex To and Jack Que evaluate the role of
encrypted messaging in modern conflict in "The Use of Telegram in the Russian-Ukrainian War." And Jason R. closes
the issue with an analysis of headline-driven media manipulation in "To What Extent Do Different News Sources Engage
in Media Manipulation Through Headlines?"

This journal represents months of dedication and collaboration between each author and their mentor. We are grateful
to our editors and editorial board for their skilled assistance and valuable insights in shaping this first issue.
